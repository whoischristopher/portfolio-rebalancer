📘 How Rebalancing Works

The plan is produced by an optimiser that weighs every possible set of trades at once and picks the cheapest one, where "cost" means:

  1. Drift outside the band (±{{ "%.2f"|format(balanced_threshold) }} pts) – by far the largest cost. Drift inside the band is nearly free: it doesn't chase exact targets.
  2. Capital gains realised by selling in non-registered accounts (using each holding's book value). Losses aren't penalised.
  3. Number of trades (plus your per-trade cost from Settings).
  4. Number of positions – new positions are discouraged, and a small, scattered position gets closed out when that's cheap.
  5. Account priority from the Securities page – a preference, not a hard rule. Restrictions are hard rules.

Always true: each account funds its own buys (no cash moves between accounts); no account's cash goes negative; currency is converted at spot rate.

Non-registered sells follow your Settings: Never, Allow, or Ask – Ask plans without them and, if something is still outside its band, shows what selling would fix and the gain it would realise.

Superficial loss rule: a security sold at a loss in a non-registered account is never bought back (in any account) in the same plan, or within 30 days of a loss sale recorded here; and a security bought in the last 30 days isn't sold at a loss. Only trades executed through this app are known – trades placed directly at your brokerage (or by your spouse) aren't visible.

Fractional final buy (Settings): leftover cash in an account goes into that account's last buy as fractional shares, without pushing any class past its band.
